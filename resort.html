Khot Juva · the walk in

Look up on the way in

Three canopies, one path.

The path from the jetty to the house runs about two hundred metres under a canopy nobody planted. Three species have grown into each other over generations — here's what you're standing under, in the order you meet it.

01

Banyan aerial roots · वड

The thick woody columns dropping straight down. Each one started as a hanging root, reached soil, and thickened into a trunk of its own — so a single banyan here reads as a whole grove.

02

Coconut palm · माड

The long feathered fronds cutting across the frame. Konkan's working tree — copra, coir, the neera tapped at dawn, and the leaf that still roofs the outbuildings.

03

Mangrove edge · खारफुटी

Where the path meets the Gad River, the canopy thins into mangrove. It is what holds the island's bank together, and where the egrets and kingfishers work the low tide.

Where we are

A real family homestead, not a resort built for one.

The Khot family has lived on this island for over four hundred years.

Manohar Resort Homestay sits on Khot Juva island in Kalavali, Malvan taluka, Sindhudurg — surrounded on every side by the Gad River. It's a working homestead first: guests are hosted by Anil Khot and family, in the same rhythm the island has kept for generations.

There's no need to dress it up. It's quiet, it's remote enough to feel like an escape, and meals come from the family kitchen rather than a restaurant menu.

On the table

A Malvani thali, cooked the way it's cooked at home.

A look around

Scroll through a morning and an evening on the island.

01 — The shallows

The shallows fill up before sunset

By late afternoon the mudflats along the Gad River draw a small crowd of egrets, picking through the shallows while the light turns from white to gold. It's a five-minute walk from the guest rooms — no boat needed, just good timing.

02 — The crossing

The family's boat, tied up for the night

The Khots still use the river the way the island always has — a wooden boat for the short crossing, moored at the jetty once the day's fishing or ferrying is done. In the blue hour, it's one of the quietest corners of the property.

03 — Evenings

Evening over the backwater

The palms on the far bank go dark first, and the water holds the colour a little longer. Most evenings, this is the view from the dining pavilion — no schedule, just whenever the sky decides to do this.

04 — Mornings

Two boats, waiting on the sunrise

Early risers get the island mostly to themselves — mist over the water, a couple of canoes pulled up on the bank, and the day's first boat traffic still an hour or two off.
